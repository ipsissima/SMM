# SMM Definitive Reconstruction — Step 2
## Canonical Neuronal Substrate

**Decision:** Use an excitatory–inhibitory next-generation neural mass model based on the Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin / QIF mean-field framework, with conductance-based synapses, as the primary neuronal substrate of the definitive SMM.

**Status:** Canonical working decision, subject only to failure of explicit parameter-recovery / identifiability tests.

---

## 1. Why the old Wilson–Cowan layer is not retained as the primary model

Wilson–Cowan remains an important phenomenological population model, but it is not the optimal foundation for the definitive SMM because:

1. its population transfer sigmoid is phenomenological rather than an exact reduction from a spiking model;
2. mapping extracellular potassium changes onto sigmoid threshold/gain would introduce an additional heuristic layer;
3. its state variables are less directly connected to a microscopic membrane-voltage description;
4. its simple form is useful as a robustness control but not as the strongest mechanistic base for a Nature Communications-level multiscale paper.

---

## 2. Why Jansen–Rit is not the primary model

Jansen–Rit has a major strength: it was designed for EEG-like activity and represents interacting pyramidal, excitatory-interneuron, and inhibitory-interneuron populations with explicit postsynaptic filters.

However:

- the firing-rate transfer remains a heuristic static sigmoid;
- parameter inference is nontrivial and can be weakly identifiable;
- mapping the astroglial potassium mechanism to its threshold/gain parameters would be less direct than in a voltage-based QIF normal form;
- it is therefore retained as an important **backbone robustness model**, not as the canonical neuronal substrate.

---

## 3. Why Stuart–Landau / Hopf is not the primary model

Stuart–Landau and related compact phenomenological oscillators can fit EEG spectra extremely well and are computationally efficient.

That is exactly why they should be used as a **strong phenomenological null**, not as the mechanistic neuronal layer.

If a simple oscillator plus generic slow modulation predicts the held-out data as well as the SMM, the data do not justify the SMM mechanism.

---

## 4. Why Larter–Breakspear is not the primary model

Larter–Breakspear is highly relevant because it explicitly contains ionic reversal potentials, including potassium, and has been used in whole-brain modelling.

Its disadvantages for the primary SMM are:

- it is a hybrid model rather than an exact population reduction;
- the inhibitory population is more phenomenological than the excitatory population;
- it has a rich/chaotic dynamical repertoire that increases inferential complexity;
- explicit ionic sensitivity can be used to calibrate or cross-check the K+-to-excitability mapping without making the entire SMM depend on this particular model.

Larter–Breakspear is therefore a **mechanistic comparator/calibration model**, not the main backbone.

---

## 5. Canonical choice: next-generation QIF neural mass

The QIF neuron is the normal form of class-I neuronal excitability near a saddle-node-on-invariant-circle transition.

For neuron i in population a (a = E or I) at cortical region r:

\[
\tau_a \dot v_{ari}
=
v_{ari}^{2}
+\eta_{ari}
+I^{\mathrm{syn}}_{ar}(t)
+I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{ar}(t)
+I^{K}_{ar}(t).
\]

For a Lorentzian distribution of intrinsic excitabilities with center \(\bar\eta_a\) and width \(\Delta_a\), the thermodynamic-limit population dynamics admit an exact low-dimensional reduction in terms of:

- instantaneous mean firing rate \(R_{ar}(t)\);
- mean membrane potential \(V_{ar}(t)\).

In current-based form:

\[
\tau_a\dot R_{ar}
=
\frac{\Delta_a}{\pi\tau_a}
+2R_{ar}V_{ar},
\]

\[
\tau_a\dot V_{ar}
=
V_{ar}^{2}
+\bar\eta_a
-(\pi\tau_aR_{ar})^{2}
+I^{\mathrm{syn}}_{ar}
+I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{ar}
+I^{K}_{ar}.
\]

This is preferred to a static sigmoid neural mass because it maintains an explicit micro-to-meso link.

---

## 6. Conductance-based synapses

The definitive model should use conductance-based synaptic currents rather than purely current-based couplings whenever computationally feasible.

For synaptic class b acting on population a:

\[
I^{\mathrm{syn}}_{ab,r}
=
G_{ab,r}(t)\,[E_{ab}-V_{a,r}(t)].
\]

With an alpha-function synapse, conductance can be generated by a second-order filter:

\[
\ddot G_{ab,r}
+2\alpha_{ab}\dot G_{ab,r}
+\alpha_{ab}^{2}G_{ab,r}
=
\alpha_{ab}^{2}\kappa_{ab}R_{b,r}.
\]

A conductance-based QIF mean field modifies both firing-rate and voltage dynamics. In compact notation, with total conductance \(G_a=\sum_bG_{ab}\):

\[
\tau_a\dot R_a
=
\frac{\Delta_a}{\pi\tau_a}
+2R_aV_a
-G_aR_a,
\]

\[
\tau_a\dot V_a
=
V_a^2
+\bar\eta_a
-(\pi\tau_aR_a)^2
+\sum_bG_{ab}(E_{ab}-V_a)
+I^{\mathrm{ext}}_a
+I^K_a.
\]

Exact coefficients/scaling will be taken from the chosen next-generation mean-field derivation and kept dimensionally explicit in implementation.

## 7. E–I cortical node

Each cortical region r contains at least:

\[
(R_{E,r},V_{E,r}),
\qquad
(R_{I,r},V_{I,r}),
\]

plus synaptic conductance/filter variables for:

- E→E,
- E→I,
- I→E,
- I→I.

The definitive core does **not require neuronal gap-junction coupling**. Keeping it absent in the core prevents confusion between neuronal electrical coupling and the astroglial connexin-mediated mesh.

Neuronal electrical coupling can be added only as a robustness extension if justified.

---

## 8. Long-range neuronal network

Long-range interactions are neuronal and predominantly excitatory.

For cortical regions r and s, structural connectivity is represented by \(W_{rs}\), with axonal delays \(T_{rs}\):

\[
T_{rs}=\frac{d_{rs}}{v_{axon}}.
\]

Long-range drive into region r has the generic form

\[
I^{\mathrm{LR}}_{E,r}(t)
=
G_N\sum_sW_{rs}\,
\mathcal S[R_{E,s}(t-T_{rs})],
\]

where \(\mathcal S\) denotes the chosen synaptic filter/conductance operator.

This gives a clean separation:

\[
\boxed{
L_g=\text{local astroglial geometry}
}
\]

versus

\[
\boxed{
W_N=\text{long-range neuronal geometry}.
}
\]

---

## 9. The physically constrained glia→neuron coupling

This is the central bridge.

Extracellular potassium determines the potassium Nernst potential:

\[
E_K(K_e)
=
\frac{RT}{F}
\ln\frac{K_e}{K_i}.
\]

Around physiological baseline \(K_{e0}\),

\[
\delta E_K
=
E_K(K_e)-E_K(K_{e0}).
\]

The resulting change in potassium current alters the distance of the neuronal membrane dynamics from firing threshold / bifurcation.

Rather than inserting an arbitrary glial gain, define a calibrated effective QIF drive:

\[
I^K_{a,r}(t)
=
\mathcal R_a[K^e_r(t)],
\]

where \(\mathcal R_a\) is obtained from a single-neuron conductance-based reduction/calibration.

Locally around baseline:

\[
I^K_{a,r}(t)
\approx
\chi_a\,[K^e_r(t)-K_{e0}],
\]

or equivalently

\[
I^K_{a,r}(t)
\approx
\tilde\chi_a\,\delta E_K(t).
\]

The coefficient is **not to be treated as an unconstrained arbitrary fit parameter**. It must be derived or assigned a biologically informed prior from the microscopic calibration.

This is how the astroglial state moves the neuronal operating point.

## 10. Slow-fast composition with the SMM

The astroglial layer evolves slowly:

\[
\dot g_r
=
\varepsilon G_r(g,X)
+\xi_{g,r},
\qquad
\varepsilon\ll1.
\]

The neuronal system evolves rapidly:

\[
\dot X_r
=
F_{\mathrm{QIF}}
\left(
X_r;
I^K[g_r],
W_N
\right)
+\xi_{N,r}.
\]

This allows an adiabatic interpretation:

1. on neuronal timescales, the glial state is approximately fixed;
2. the neuronal operator is therefore \(A(g)\);
3. as glial homeostasis slowly changes, the neuronal operating point, poles/eigenvalues, gain, synchrony and metastability change.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
A_0\rightarrow A(g)
}
\]

is no longer merely conceptual: it has a defined cellular route through extracellular K+ and neuronal excitability.

## 11. Kuramoto is now an observable, not a layer

The QIF/MPR mean-field variables can be mapped to a complex synchrony/order parameter through the known Möbius transform.

Therefore the definitive SMM can quantify within-population synchrony without introducing an independent Kuramoto oscillator system.

This preserves the useful synchrony mathematics of the old SMM while removing its redundant third neuronal ontology.

---

## 12. EEG observation model

The measured EEG must be generated by a distinct observation layer.

For cortical region r, define a source/dipole proxy from net postsynaptic/transmembrane currents in the aligned excitatory/pyramidal population:

\[
q_r(t)
=
\sum_b c_{b,r}
G_{Eb,r}(t)
[E_b-V_{E,r}(t)].
\]

The coefficients/signs encode the effective dendritic source geometry.

Scalp EEG is then:

\[
\boxed{
y(t)=Lq(t)+\epsilon(t)
}
\]

where \(L\) is a lead-field/forward operator and \(\epsilon\) measurement noise.

The paper must not equate firing rate or glial field directly with scalp EEG.

## 13. Strong model-comparison hierarchy

All principal comparisons should use the **same neuronal backbone** wherever possible.

### M0 — Neural only
QIF/MPR E–I network with no slow controller.

### M1 — Non-spatial generic slow control
Same neuronal network + independent OU-like slow parameter drift.

### M2 — Spatial generic slow field
Same neuronal network + spatially coupled slow latent field, without astroglial mechanistic constraints.

### M3 — Definitive SMM
Same neuronal network + astroglial K+-homeostasis field with physiologically constrained topology, timescales and K→neuronal mapping.

### M4 — SMM + Ca/IP3
Only if empirically justified.

### M5 — SMM + gliotransmission
Only if empirically justified.

This hierarchy ensures that any SMM advantage cannot be attributed simply to adding a slow latent variable.

---

## 14. Strong alternative-backbone tests

To establish that a positive result is not peculiar to one neuronal model:

1. **Jansen–Rit** should be used as the principal mechanistic robustness backbone.
2. **Stuart–Landau/Hopf** should be used as a strong phenomenological spectral null because compact oscillators can fit EEG spectra very well.
3. **Larter–Breakspear** can be used to cross-check the direction/magnitude of potassium sensitivity and bifurcation effects.

The primary inferential claims remain based on the QIF/MPR system.

---

## 15. Why this choice is especially suitable for the definitive SMM

The QIF/MPR backbone uniquely gives the project all of the following at once:

- an explicit microscopic spiking origin;
- an exact mean-field reduction under stated assumptions;
- mean membrane potential;
- mean firing rate;
- derived synchrony/order parameter;
- conductance-based synapses with reversal potentials;
- E/I cortical nodes;
- compatibility with whole-brain structural connectivity and delays;
- low enough dimensionality for parameter recovery and model comparison;
- a natural slow control port through excitability/input;
- a viable route to an explicit EEG forward model.

This is substantially stronger than retaining the old Wilson–Cowan + Kuramoto architecture.

---

## 16. Immediate technical consequence: the K→QIF map must be derived before whole-brain fitting

The next substep is **2A: microscopic calibration**.

We must take a conductance-based class-I neuronal model with explicit extracellular K+, compute

\[
K_e \mapsto E_K \mapsto \text{bifurcation/threshold shift},
\]

and derive/calibrate the corresponding QIF effective input

\[
K_e \mapsto I^K.
\]

Only after this mapping is fixed or strongly constrained may the astroglial field be coupled to the population model.

This prevents the central glia→neuron coupling from becoming a free phenomenological knob.

## 17. Key literature anchors for this decision

- Montbrió, Pazó & Roxin (2015): exact macroscopic description of QIF networks.
- Coombes / Byrne and subsequent next-generation neural-mass work: conductance-based and synaptic extensions.
- Clusella et al. (2023, Biological Cybernetics): exact QIF mean field with second-order synapses versus heuristic static-transfer neural masses.
- Forrester et al. (2024, PLOS Computational Biology): E–I next-generation neural mass in a 68-region human whole-brain network with structural connectivity and delays.
- Omejc et al. (2026, PLOS Computational Biology): broad EEG population-model benchmark; generalized MPR among the best-performing models for fit/stability/simplicity, and demonstration that spectra alone do not identify a unique mechanism.
- Chesebro et al. (2023): Larter–Breakspear ion-gradient analysis as a useful ionic-sensitivity comparator.
- Hübel et al. (2021): extracellular potassium can qualitatively change neuronal excitability/bifurcation class.

## 18. Canonical Step-2 decision

> **The definitive SMM will use a conductance-based excitatory–inhibitory next-generation QIF/MPR neural mass as its primary neuronal substrate. Astroglial potassium homeostasis will couple into this system through a microscopically calibrated slow excitability/input term, while synchrony will be derived from the neuronal state and EEG will be generated through a separate current-dipole/lead-field observation model.**
